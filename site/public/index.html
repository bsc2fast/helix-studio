See the burn. Then send it.

Helix Studio is a small web app that runs on your own computer. Drop in a PDF, lay it out on a true-to-scale picture of the bed and your stock, see exactly how it will burn on the material you've loaded — then engrave and cut in one job, straight to the laser over your network. No VisiCut, no Windows-only driver, no print queue, no cloud.

The burn preview

Not the PDF — what the laser will leave

The bed doesn't show your page on white paper. It shows the material, in its colour, with your artwork burned into it the way this preset will burn it.

  • Honest about what burns. The engraving is 1-bit: every pixel that isn't near-white burns at full power. Greys and colours all burn alike, and the preview shows exactly that.
  • Harder presets look harder. Power ÷ speed (× DPI) sets how dark, deep and charred the burn looks — frosted white on acrylic, a brown scorch on wood.
  • Cut lines in the burn colour, slowly pulsing so you can't miss them.
  • Knows vector from picture. Each page says whether it's pure vector, and the resolution of any picture in it — amber below 150 dpi, before it burns blocky.

Pick it visually

Every choice is a picture, not a list

Each dropdown opens a full-window picker. Materials come in the colours they're sold in, thicknesses are drawn edge-on at real size, and presets show a sample burn.

Engrave + cut New

Engrave it and cut it out, in one job

Pick an Engrave + cut preset and the engraving and the cut go to the laser together — engraving first, then the cut, so the piece is only freed at the end. One GO does both.

  • One per engraving style, each paired with the thinnest rated cut that goes through your stock. Too thick to cut? The pairs simply aren't offered.
  • Draw cut lines as hairlines (0.1 mm or thinner — Illustrator's 0.001 pt works). Everything else engraves; the hairlines are kept out of the engraving.
  • No hairlines, no send. If a page has nothing to cut, it says so and Send stays off.

New — check it on your machine first. Run the included engrave-cut-test.pdf on scrap: the piece should come out 50.0 mm with the engraved square 30.0 mm, 10.0 mm in from every edge.

Job time

Know how long it'll take before you send

The Send button shows an estimate worked out from the job exactly as the laser will run it.

  • Engraving: the rows the head really sweeps and how far — the driver trims each row to its burn and engraves both ways, and the estimate counts the same thing.
  • Cuts: the length of every path and how many there are.
  • Learns your machine. After a job, type how long it really took; every later estimate uses it. Until a cut has been timed, cut estimates show a ~.

Placement

Put it exactly where the stock is

  • Corner and Center for the stock and the artwork — pick a corner on the bed and it snaps there.
  • Page layout. Design on an artboard the size of your stock and the artwork keeps its spot on it; a page that matches the chosen stock lands on it by itself.
  • Rulers on all four sides — from the home corner, and from the far one.
  • Several PDFs, copies, rotation, your own stock sizes, and mm or inches.

Guards

Mistakes stop at the screen

  • A safety boundary you calibrate once. Artwork across it turns red and won't send.
  • Overlaps caught before they're burned twice — and the server checks again, so a script can't slip one through.
  • Send says what's wrong. Hover it: no preset, a bad placement, or the laser not on the network.
  • Knows when the laser is there. A live dot by its IP; if it drops, the chip turns into Scan and finds it on your network.

Make it yours

Your stock, your colours, your units

  • Add the stock you actually buy — its colour and the colour a burn leaves on it. The text on it picks its own contrast.
  • Your own stock sizes, offered only for their material.
  • Millimetres or inches, on screen only — everything is stored and sent in mm.
  • Remembers your setup, so the next session opens where you left off.

How it works

From PDF to laser, on your own machine

An Epilog on the network already speaks a printing protocol. Helix Studio builds the job itself and sends it straight there — nothing else to install between you and the machine.

Any operating system

The interface is a browser page, so macOS, Windows and Linux all work the same way. No driver, no queue.

The datasheet, built in

Epilog's published settings for a 30 W tube ship as presets per material; cuts that can't get through your thickness are hidden.

Scriptable

A small REST API: import a PDF, place pages, send — the same safety and overlap checks apply.

Get started

Running in about five minutes

  1. Install Poppler

    It renders the PDF — the only dependency that isn't Python.

    # macOS
brew install poppler

# Windows (then reopen the terminal)
winget install --id oschwartz10612.Poppler

# Debian / Ubuntu
sudo apt install poppler-utils

  2. Get Helix Studio

    git clone https://github.com/bsc2fast/helix-studio.git
cd helix-studio
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt

  3. Point it at your machine

    Copy config.example.json to config.json and set your laser's IP address and your own bed measurements. The shipped calibration belongs to one specific machine — not yours. If the laser isn't where the config says, the IP chip turns into Scan and looks for it.

  4. Run it

    python3 server.py      # then open http://localhost:4060

⚠ About running a laser

A laser cutter is a fire hazard and a machine that can hurt you. Never run a job unattended; keep the lid closed and extraction on.

The presets are Epilog's published starting points for a 30 W tube — your machine's real power, optics and material differ, so test on scrap first. The burn preview and time estimates are guides, not promises. The bed calibration that ships with the code belongs to one specific machine; measure your own before the first send. The software refuses out-of-bounds jobs, but that guard is only as good as the numbers you give it.
